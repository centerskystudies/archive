Son of the master craftsman Daedalus, who constructed wings of wax and feather to escape the labyrinth of King Minos. Daedalus warned his son: don't fly too low (the sea's moisture would weigh the wings down) and don't fly too high (the sun's heat would melt the wax). Icarus flew too high. The wax melted. He fell into the sea.

The myth is older than aviation and newer than yesterday. It is the foundational story of technological overreach — the hubris of those who reach for the sky without respect for its conditions.

## The Modern Mirror

On April 28, 2021, NASA's Parker Solar Probe became the first spacecraft to "touch" the sun — flying through a coronal mass ejection. The probe was named for Eugene "Gene" Parker, the astrophysicist who first proposed the existence of solar wind in 1958. He lived to see the launch of his namesake spacecraft before passing in 2022 at 94. Where Icarus failed in myth, Parker succeeded in science — through calculation instead of hubris.

## Cultural Residue

The myth has entered everyday language: "don't fly too close to the sun" is spoken daily by people who have never read Ovid. It appears in boardrooms, therapy sessions, and parenting advice. It is one of the most durable warning labels in human culture.
